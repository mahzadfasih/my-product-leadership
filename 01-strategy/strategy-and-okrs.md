# Product Strategy & OKRs: Meridian Foundations

> Module 1 · Write a Strategy That Can Survive a Test

## 1. Playing to Win cascade

| Question | Your choice |
|---|---|
| **Winning aspiration** | A mobile and tablet app that job site workers can use to take pictures, write comments, and provide updates and reports ad-hoc. The submitted tickets should integrate with existing solutions to enable office-field collaboration. This will save time for field staff from duplicated work of re-submitting issues into a system once they are at a laptop. |
| **Where to play** | Mid-size construction companies in U.S. who have projects of $5M - $50M. Global rollout will be a future consideration. |
| **How to win** | Years of construction project data to understand what field issues require to be tracked and communicated. We also have the integrated office-field collaboration workflows that helps customers save time in submissions. |
| **Capabilities required** | A ticketing system that allows text and media submissions, plus managing permissions and ownership of tickets. The app is integrated with the management tools to consolidated silo'd workflows. |
| **Management systems** | Number of customers (weekly active) to understand enterprise adoption and MAU per customer to understand if the app is usable to field agents. |

## 2. Your one hard no

> **Hard no:** We will not support small businesses (projects of <$5M) given that their processes are more flat and don't require a integrated ticketing system. For these customers, Whatsapp is already a sufficient solution and we will not bring any competitive advantage in the space.

## 3. OKR cascade

> **Objective:** Become the in-field app for mid-size construction companies.
>
> - **KR1:** Adoption: 50% of existing customers adopt the mobile app by end of year.
> - **KR2:** Stickiness: MAU grows 10% month over month by end of year.
> - **KR3:** Stickiness: Number of tickets submitted through the app grows 50% month over month by end of year.

## 4. AI pressure-test

**Most valid challenge:** My strategy defined global rollout as a hard no.  per AI's feedback, the commercial rollout can be a future opportunity, but if we call it out of scope, we are blocking ourselves out of having a solution that can support regionalization and translations in future.

**What I will change and what I will defend:** Changed: the out of scope changed from global footprint to customer size. Defend: AI suggested that KRs are too aggressive. Given the existing footprint, I believe the numbers are realistic. This app is not only replacing an app for field staff, but also connecting them to management with integrated workflows. For that reason, management support will also drive adoption based on mandate for using the tool.
